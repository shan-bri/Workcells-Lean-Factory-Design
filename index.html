The brief

Breezeway Fans Ltd (fictional) makes Desk and Tower fans for the domestic market, and Wall and Duct fans for industry. Suppose Breezeway goes ahead with a focused factory for domestic fans. Inside it, the work is done in two linked workcells:

  • Machining & winding cell: automatic, single-cycle machines that make the motor housing and wind the motor. A worker unloads, loads and starts each machine (30 s per machine).
  • Assembly & test cell: manual stations that assemble, test and pack the fan.

Pressed blanks come from the shared press outside the focused factory (a special operation), so you can assume they are always available. The two cells are linked by kanban: the machining cell only makes a housing when there is space in the kanban rack.

Walking

Stations are 2 m apart around a U. Walking time = distance (m) at 1 m/s + 2 s to pick up or put down. The simulator works these out from the layout; the times are shown on each worker’s loop in “Check your working”.

Costs

Labour £18 per worker-hour. Running cost (energy, tooling, depreciation): assembly cell £30/h, machining cell £95/h. A second motor winder costs £45,000.

Your tasks

  1. Takt. What is the required cycle time (takt) for the domestic focused factory?
  2. One-worker assembly cell. Work out the cell CT and capacity per day with one worker, by hand. Then build it in tab 2 and check.
  3. Staffing. What is the minimum number of workers to meet takt? Assign stations to workers (subcells) and compare with the rabbit chase. Which is better here, and why?
  4. Machining cell. Work out the worker CT and the longest machine CT. Does adding a second worker help? What would?
  5. Batch size and SMED. Keep sequential changeover. Try a batch of one (D T D T D), small batches and daily batches. How much SMED is needed before a batch of one works? Extension: switch to stopping the whole cell. Why does it hurt small batches far more than daily ones?
  6. Staffing and cost. Use tab 4 to compare unit costs for different numbers of workers at £18/h and £24/h. Is the cheapest cell per fan always the right choice?
  7. Round 2. Link the cells in tab 5 and face +15% demand, variable task times and winder breakdowns. Recover with the fewest extra workers and the least spending, then pitch your design.

Predict first, then simulate. Write your hand calculation in “Check your working” before you run the simulator: the point is to see whether the lecture formulas hold up, and where they don’t.
